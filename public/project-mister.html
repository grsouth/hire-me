Building a humidity-controlled gecko misting system

This project started as a simple goal: keep a gecko enclosure consistently humid without manual spraying. It evolved into a small automation system that reads a DHT22 sensor, controls a 12V pump, and logs humidity over time for tuning.

Hardware: Arduino Nano, DHT22, 12V pump Fabrication: 3D-printed mounts + tubing clips Focus: safe automation and reliability

What I built

The controller reads humidity every few seconds and opens a relay when the enclosure dips below a target range. A rolling average prevents rapid cycling, and a minimum cooldown keeps the pump healthy.

The plumbing mounts are printed in PETG, and the tubing route keeps mist away from electronics. A small status LED provides at-a-glance feedback during maintenance.

What I learned

Sensor placement was critical. Moving the DHT22 away from the mist nozzle and adding a mesh guard made the readings far more stable.

The logging step was a bonus: by capturing humidity curves, I could tune the trigger range to balance the reptile's needs and water usage.
